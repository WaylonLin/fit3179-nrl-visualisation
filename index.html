Seventeen clubs. One ladder. Very different stories.
The final standings tell us who finished ahead. They leave out where teams played, how they performed, and how quickly their fortunes could turn.

The top four did not share one performance profile. Compare five measures across the 2025 regular season. Switch between rank and actual per-match values. Ranks show ordering, not the size of the gaps between clubs.

Chapter 01

01 Ground

Where the competition plays, and why home can mean several venues.

Hosting is concentrated in the east.

New South Wales and Queensland staged 169 of Australia's 199 NRL fixtures in 2025 (84.9%). Adjusting for population gives a different perspective: the ACT leads in fixtures hosted per resident.

What this showsThe ACT hosted 13 fixtures for 484,630 residents: 2.68 per 100,000. Its small administrative population makes this rate sensitive to the boundary. The map measures hosting intensity only; it says nothing about fan demand, catchments or accessibility.

Sources: NRL results, ABS population and boundaries. Specification · Data

A few venues carry much of the fixture list.

The competition used 26 venues across Australia, New Zealand and the United States. Circle area represents fixture count, including finals and every game in multi-match events.

What this showsSuncorp hosted 30 fixtures, CommBank 23 and Accor 19. Together they staged 72 of 213 games (33.8%). These are games hosted, not attendance or unique event-days.

Sources: NRL results, venue coordinates and Natural Earth. Specification · Data

One club. More than one meaning of home.

Start with all 24 regular-season fixtures, then select Home fixtures to isolate Penrith’s 12 designated home games. Its established home was under redevelopment: eight home games went to CommBank; Bathurst, Mudgee, Suncorp and Las Vegas hosted one each.

What this shows“Home” is a fixture designation, not a guarantee of one stadium. Compare Penrith with Brisbane (all 12 home games at Suncorp) and the Warriors (10 at Mt Smart, plus Christchurch and Magic Round). Every club played 24 regular-season games.

Sources: NRL fixtures, club home-ground announcements (Penrith, Brisbane, Warriors). Specification · Data

Chapter 02

02 Game

How attack, defence and scoring patterns reveal differences hidden by the ladder.

More metres usually accompany a win.
Close games are less clear-cut.

Across 2023–2025, winners outgained their opponents in 75.9% of decisive games (462/609). In games decided by 1–12 points, that fell to 59.2% (189/319).

What this showsIn 130 of 319 close games, the winner did not gain more run metres. Territory and scores are associated over the same match; this is neither a forecast nor evidence that extra metres caused the result.

Sources: Champion Data, NRL results. Specification · Data

The turnaround was mostly at the defensive end.

Canterbury rose from 15th to third. Its season point difference—points scored minus points conceded—improved by 451 between 2023 and 2025.

What this showsFewer points conceded account for 78.7% of that improvement. Scoring rose from 438 to 534; conceded points fell from 769 to 414. This is an exact accounting decomposition, not an estimate of causal effects.

Source: NRL results. Specification · Data

Less dangerous progress. Not simply fewer missed tackles.

Canterbury went from conceding 6.67 line breaks a match in 2023 to 3.54 in 2025, the fewest in the league. Opponents' run metres also fell, from 1,607 to 1,336 per match: 271 fewer metres and 3.13 fewer line breaks than in 2023.

What this showsIn 2024, Canterbury conceded the fewest line breaks while recording the most missed tackles. Missed tackles alone do not describe defensive effectiveness: whether opponents turn opportunities into dangerous progress matters too. These summaries cannot isolate tactics, personnel or causality.

Source: Champion Data. Specification · Data

The most points. Still outside the top eight.

The Dolphins scored a league-leading 721 points but finished ninth, winning 12 and losing 12. Orange highlights their scoring distribution. The ladder rewards match results, not total points scored; a large win still counts as one win.

What this showsThe Dolphins averaged 41.4 points in their wins, the highest winning-game average in the league. Big scoring days inflated their season total; points could not be carried into the next game. They also conceded 596 points. Scoring dispersion provides context, not a complete explanation for ninth place.

Source: NRL results. Specification · Data

Chapter 03

03 Outcome

How half-time leads, ladder positions and finals results can tell different stories.

Most leads held. One in five reversed.

Of the 186 games with a half-time leader in 2025, 147 ended with that team winning (79.0%). In 38 (20.4%), the trailing side came back to win. One ended in a draw.

What this shows89 of 109 home leads and 58 of 77 away leads became wins. The 18 half-time ties led to eight home wins and ten away wins. The single final draw followed an away half-time lead.

Source: NRL half-time and full-time scores. Specification · Data

Last in round 12. Fourth in round 23. Seventh at the end.

A final ladder hides the journey. Penrith won four of its first 12 games, then nine of its last 12—enough to turn a season near the bottom into a finals appearance.

What this showsPenrith's recovery was substantial, but not a straight climb. Its final seventh place also understated how close it would come in the finals: a two-point preliminary-final loss to Brisbane.

Source: NRL round-by-round ladder. Specification · Data

Canberra finished first.
Brisbane lifted the trophy.

The minor premiership goes to the club that tops the regular-season ladder. The premiership goes to the grand-final winner. In 2025, Canberra won the first distinction; fourth-placed Brisbane won the second.

Opponents: CAN Canberra · MEL Melbourne · CBY Canterbury · BRI Brisbane · CRO Cronulla · NZ Warriors · PEN Penrith · SYD Roosters.

What this showsBrisbane won its three finals by just 1, 2 and 4 points. Canberra's top seed earned a second chance, but two defeats ended its season. Consistency across the regular season and winning the finals are different achievements.

Source: NRL finals results. Specification · Data

Beyond the ladder

Read the position.
Then look at the story.

The NRL ladder is a useful summary. But a fuller account needs the grounds, the balance of attack and defence, the spread of performances, and the moments when an advantage changed hands.

Sources & methods

Analysis centres on the 2025 men's NRL season, with 2023–2025 regular-season comparisons where stated. The pinned results source has a complete 2025 season and incomplete 2026 coverage; 2025 is the latest complete season in that snapshot.

  • Results and ladders: Useless NRL Stats, pinned 20 June 2026 snapshot, based on Rugby League Project. Accessed 6 October 2026.
  • Team metrics: Champion Data public match feed, using the endpoint documented by nrlR. Accessed 11 October 2026. All 612 regular-season matches join on teams, date and score. The nrlR code licence does not establish a licence for the underlying provider data; public-reuse status remains unconfirmed.
  • Population and boundaries: based on ABS Regional Population 2024–25, Table 1, preliminary 30 June 2025 ERP; ABS ASGS 2025 LGA boundaries. Matching LGAs aggregated to states/territories. Geometry simplified for display; source assignments retained.
  • Map context: Natural Earth country boundaries; Wikipedia contributors for venue coordinates, with per-venue revision links in the venue data. Club anchor definitions and sources are in the anchor data.
